# What mdown is — in plain English

## The short version

It's a **"Save As" button for video and audio**.

Think of a VCR. You could record a show onto a tape and watch it later at home.
Nobody called that stealing — you were copying something already being broadcast
into your living room, for yourself. This is the modern version, for links
instead of TV channels.

## What it does

You paste a link. It fetches the file and hands it to you. That's the whole
feature set.

## What it does not do — the important part

- **It stores nothing.** There is no library of films on a server. Each file
  exists for a few minutes, goes to one person, and is deleted. Nothing is
  re-published or shared.
- **It unlocks nothing.** No Netflix, no Spotify, no Disney+, no paid
  subscriptions, no logins. Those are deliberately locked and this tool simply
  fails on them. It cannot break encryption and isn't built to try.
- **It can't redistribute.** There is no "share this with others" button,
  because handing out copies is the part that is actually illegal. The tool has
  no ability to do it.
- **It sells nothing and shows no ads.** It costs about $13 a year to run, which
  is why there is a small server. Nobody profits from anyone else's work.

## The honest bit about the law

What is clearly illegal is **distributing** — uploading someone's film and giving
copies to strangers, or selling them. That is piracy, and this cannot do it.

What is grey is **personal copying** — saving something to watch later. That
varies by country and by site. Many websites have a house rule in their terms
saying "please don't download". That is like a "no outside food" sign: breaking
it can get your account in trouble with that website, but it is not a crime.

So the fair description: **a convenience tool for personal use, not a
distribution service.** The person using it is responsible for what they save,
the same way owning a tape recorder doesn't make you responsible for what
somebody records.

## Why it needs a computer somewhere

The little website is just the remote control. The actual work — fetching the
file, stitching the audio and video together — happens on a rented computer,
like a kitchen doing the chopping. It hands you the plate and throws away the
scraps.

## One line to take away

It is a tool. It doesn't break locks, it doesn't keep or share anything, and it
doesn't make money off anyone's work.
